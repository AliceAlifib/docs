title: "Quickstart"

---

Setup is a conversation with your own coding agent. It reads your repos, asks what it could not read, and writes a first draft of your project. You correct the draft.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the invite">
    Open [alicein.app/start](https://alicein.app/start) and sign in with Google. There is no separate token to copy.

    You land on **Connect**. The workspace already exists, and the command on the page already carries your token. Treat that command like a password: do not paste it into a chat or a ticket.

    Bookmark [alicein.app/workspace](https://alicein.app/workspace). That is the map. It starts empty until you onboard.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect your agent">
    Have Claude Code, Codex, or Grok Build installed and signed in first, on your own account. You also need Node 18 or newer.

    On the Connect page, pick **macOS / Linux** or **Windows**, press **Copy command**, and paste it into a terminal *inside the repo you work in* — not your home folder, not the browser's address bar.

    Check it took: run `/mcp` in Claude Code and you should see **alicein**. In Grok, `grok inspect`. Full detail, including adding a second repo later, in [Connect your agent](/using/connect-your-agent).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Let it onboard you">
    In that session:

    ```text
    /alice-onboard
    ```

    It lists your repos, asks which belong to this project, reads them on your machine, then interviews you in short rounds. Each question comes with the answer it would give, so you can agree or correct.

    You approve three things before they save: **what the project is**, **the plan** (phases and enablers), and **the factory map**. See [Onboarding](/using/onboarding).

    If you already track work in Linear, say so in the interview and it comes across in the same session.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Correct it on the map">
    Open [alicein.app/workspace](https://alicein.app/workspace), or refresh it if it is already open. Your agent should also hand you this link when onboarding finishes.

    You land on [the project map](/using/project-map). Phases run left to right — scroll or zoom out for the later ones. Each enabler shows cards done out of total; click it to see the cards. Drag, rename, and delete until the draft is yours.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Look around">
    Three views and a chat:

    - **[The project map](/using/project-map)** — your plan: phases, enablers and cards.
    - **[The factory map](/using/factory-map)** — your system: what its parts are and how they connect. Off until you turn **Show factory tab** on in Settings.
    - **[The knowledge view](/using/knowledge)** — everything your project knows, and what led to what.
    - **[Alice](/using/alice-chat)**, bottom right — "where are we?", "what needs me?", "why did we do X?"

    [Needs you](/using/needs-you) is the one queue where things wait for your judgment. Press `?` for the [keyboard shortcuts](/using/shortcuts).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Hand it work">
    Press **Queue for agent** on a card. In a terminal, say **"check my alicein queue"** — the card arrives with its full context pack, and comes back to you for review. See [the agent queue](/using/agent-queue).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Your first hour

1. Onboard, and answer the *why* questions yourself.
2. Fix the map: right phases, right names, nothing made up.
3. Ask your agent what to do next.
4. Queue one card and watch it come back for review.

## What to expect

- **Your agent runs on your machine and your own subscription.** We never see it or spend it.
- **The reading step is local.** No model sees your code but your own agent.
- **Your workspace is isolated per account.** We export or delete everything on request.
